# Sound prompts — exactly 500 words each

**Reference briefs only:** these exceed ElevenLabs' 500-character field. Use the [500-character prompts](AUDIO_PROMPTS_500_CHARACTERS.md) for that input.

All six prompt bodies below contain **exactly 500 words each**. The headings, duration notes and file names are outside that count. Counts use whitespace-separated words. Each linked plain text file contains only its 500-word prompt, ready to copy.

These are earlier reference briefs. Supplied effects are installed in v151; see [Sound assets](SOUND_ASSETS.md). Durations describe the desired audible event, not a guaranteed generator clip length. Longer silent tails can be trimmed.

Other versions: [short prompts](AUDIO_PROMPTS.md) · [original detailed prompts](AUDIO_PROMPTS_DETAILED.md).

## 1. Hull hit

Target event: **0.30–0.40 seconds** · Intended audio file: `player-hull-hit.wav`

[Copy the 500-word prompt](artifacts/audio-prompts-500/hull-hit.txt).

Create one original, premium science fiction spacecraft hull damage sound for Starfighter Last Signal. This optional combat asset marks a loss of hull integrity after an enemy weapon penetrates protection. Make the consequence unmistakable: a compact metallic crack attached to a deep descending electronic thud. The ship remains alive and maneuverable. Deliver one isolated event lasting between 0.30 and 0.40 seconds, with immediate onset and a short tail.

Begin with a hard, dry contact transient: a thick alloy plate snapping inward under localized pressure. Combine a brief jagged metallic bite with a muted mechanical knock, giving the front edge physical resistance rather than a thin click. Let a rough fracture texture follow the strike, suggesting stressed rivets and compressed structure without becoming loose debris, scattered impacts, or a shower of fragments. Keep the attack readable at modest playback volume.

Directly beneath the crack, add a dense synthesized impact body with a rapid downward pitch bend. Start in the low midrange, descend toward a firm bass center, and stop before it becomes a cinematic subwoofer boom. The falling contour should communicate danger and sudden energy loss. Its body must remain audible on laptop speakers through restrained upper harmonics, not depend entirely on low frequencies. Avoid a sustained musical note or recognizable tonal interval.

Bind the metal and electronic layers into one contact, with no audible delay between them. A short gritty resonance may imply a damaged internal panel vibrating once, but suppress rattling sequences and repeated bounces. Shape the body with a quick initial compression, a small weighty bloom, and decisive damping. Give the electronic component a torn edge, like voltage briefly collapsing through armored machinery, while retaining a clean and intelligible silhouette.

The emotional character is urgent, consequential, and controlled. This is personal damage to the player's vessel, felt close to the cockpit without sounding like a recorded cockpit ambience. Do not imply catastrophic destruction, a reactor breach, an explosion, ship breakup, a death sequence, or an external battlefield. Exclude whooshing projectiles, enemy firing, incoming travel, warning alarms, speech, music, crowd noise, room tone, and environmental beds.

Keep the recording dry and centered. Use no discernible room, cavern, hangar, long reverb, or stereo echo. If subtle stereo texture adds material depth, anchor the attack and bass firmly in the center and preserve mono compatibility. Avoid excessive width, brittle treble, clipping, and uncontrolled distortion. Leave headroom and retain a strong transient without a painful peak.

Make this clearly heavier and rougher than a shield impact. There must be no rounded protective energy cushion, upward glass shimmer, sparkling magical flourish, or tiny successful block tick. The dominant signature is solid metal yielding followed by a dark electronic drop.

End with a rapidly damped trace of strained metal and cooling circuitry, disappearing cleanly into silence. Do not append a second strike, recharge sound, recovery chime, trailing hiss, or mechanical reset. Deliver a finished, usable gameplay effect whose short silhouette remains distinct during rapid shooting and whose repeated playback feels tactile rather than fatiguing.

Add a faint, smooth electronic body underneath the transient, more felt as a soft pulse than heard as a note. Favor a compact midrange presence with minimal bass. A tiny downward relaxation can imply energy being absorbed, but avoid an obvious pitch sweep, melodic interval, rising charge, or cartoon zap.

The protective surface should feel clean, controlled, and slightly alien through subtle spectral irregularity. Use delicate inharmonic texture rather than a ringing metal resonance. Nothing should imply mechanical damage or material fracture. Exclude debris, sparks scattering outward, crackling aftereffects, and any impact thud that would make the block sound destructive.

Keep the decay nearly instantaneous and completely dry. End every layer inside the specified duration, with no audible reverb, echo, room tone, or lingering electronic whine. Leave space for weapon attacks and enemy damage cues. Avoid subsonic weight, excessive stereo width, distortion, heavy compression pumping, and dramatic cinematic emphasis.

Render only one event, not a sequence, demonstration, or collection of alternatives. Keep onset timing stable and avoid large changes in pitch or loudness that suggest different gameplay outcomes.

Deliver a tightly trimmed sound effect with an immediate onset and a short, dry ending. Include no music, speech, vocalizations, warning tones, alarms, ambience, background battle, or additional actions. Lead with a small, dull tap rather than a bright clang. Imagine compact, dense alien metal struck across a textured outer plate. The attack should have a slightly blunt edge and a close, tactile character. Avoid a powerful explosive onset, hard ricochet ping, exaggerated shield bounce, or hollow industrial boom.

Build the transient from a short contact impulse with rounded high frequencies. Preserve a clear point of impact, but keep the peak controlled enough for repeated weapon contact. Layer a tiny granular scrape just behind the tap, suggesting the projectile dragging briefly across a rough surface before its energy dissipates.

Make the scrape dry, contained, and irregular at a microscopic scale. A few grains of friction can imply a coating without becoming falling debris. Avoid long grinding, tearing metal, snapping fragments, or sparkling shrapnel. The surface remains functional, although the contact feels consequential and cannot be mistaken for a miss.

Give the body a low midrange density, with a faint, muted metallic complexity above it. Use short inharmonic resonances whose partials die together quickly. Do not establish a stable musical note. Exclude ringing bowls, bells, bright steel pipes, deep drumlike cavities, and any resonance that extends beyond the requested duration.

Differentiate this armor response from a bright exposed core or hull impact. Reduce glittering treble and emphasize mass, friction, and dull resistance. Still retain a small abrasive edge so players hear that damage occurred. The result should suggest mitigation, never complete invulnerability, a magical cancellation, or a projectile deflected away.

Keep the event short, with the tap and scrape integrated into a single gesture. Use a fast decay that contracts into silence instead of opening into a larger aftermath. Apply micro fades where necessary to eliminate accidental editing clicks. Include no preattack movement, delayed secondary knock, or separate settling sound.

Present the impact close and dry, without reverberation or an implied room. Keep stereo placement narrow and compatible with positional playback. Avoid wide metallic washes, whooshes, cinematic bass reinforcement, excessive saturation, and compression that exaggerates the tail. Possible variation can come from differences in scrape grain, contact angle, muted resonance, or transient softness. Maintain the same material, impact scale, and reduced damage meaning across those possibilities. Produce only one event rather than a series of variants. Avoid random loudness jumps or pitch changes that imply critical damage.

Deliver a trimmed, immediate armor contact with a dull dense tap, a trace of gritty friction, and a short dry tail. Include no music, voice, alarm, ambience, background weapons, engine sound, or additional impacts. Its final character should be solid, alien, restrained, and suitable for repeated hits against protective plating.

## 6. Boss core / weapon assembly break

Target event: **0.28–0.40 seconds** · Intended audio file: `boss-core-break.wav`

[Copy the 500-word prompt](artifacts/audio-prompts-500/core-break.txt).

Create one original weapon assembly break sound for Starfighter Last Signal, a space shooter. Target an audible duration between 0.28 and 0.40 seconds. Express a vulnerable core or attached weapon mechanism becoming disabled through a metal crystal snap, brief pressurized energy burst, deep descending resonance, and small rising energy glint.

Start at structural failure, without a charge buildup, countdown, warning, intake, or dramatic pause. Make the leading transient a tightly joined fracture of stressed alloy and hard crystalline material. It should feel tactile and substantial, with a definite break line, rather than a generic gunshot, glass smash, or noisy explosion.

Layer the snap with a short release of contained pressure, like energized coolant escaping through a fractured chamber. Keep this burst controlled. Give it a dense center and a narrow spray of texture that vanishes quickly. Avoid long hissing vents, roaring flame, debris showers, and successive bursts suggesting continuing destruction.

Under the fracture, introduce a deep resonant body that bends downward as the mechanism loses power. Favor a damped metallic cavity response with an electrical undertone. The descent should communicate mass disengaging and energy collapsing, without an exaggerated cinematic bass drop, prolonged subwoofer rumble, or sweeping science fiction engine shutdown.

Add a small rising energy glint close to the rupture, suggesting released charge skimming across the broken core. Keep this detail secondary to the snap and descending body. Its upward movement must be brief and continuous, not a melody, victory flourish, reward arpeggio, sparkling treasure sound, or separate interface notification.

Organize all layers as one connected physical event. The snap leads, the pressure release expands immediately beneath it, and the descending resonance carries the decay. Let the rising glint cross that motion for contrast without creating a second impact. Maintain a clear hierarchy even when the sound is played quietly.

Provide low frequency weight that translates on headphones and small speakers through audible lower midrange harmonics. Keep the transient readable above weapon fire, but smooth harsh upper peaks and avoid broadband clipping. Preserve dynamic contrast between the initial fracture and its collapsing body, with no artificial pumping or crushed tail.

Keep the recording perspective dry, close, and centered, with minimal width and mono compatibility. Avoid distant battlefield reflections, reverb chambers, stereo flybys, environmental echoes, or expansive destruction tails. Spatial placement can be supplied by the game. The asset itself should contain the material response and its tightly controlled energy release.

Make the gameplay meaning a weapon assembly becoming unavailable after effective core damage. It can feel decisive, but must stay smaller than a boss death, whole ship explosion, reactor catastrophe, or level completion moment. Distinguish it from regular armor impacts, hull hits, shield breaks, and the shorter exposed core hit.

Allow imagined variation in fracture grain, pressure density, or resonance depth while preserving the same envelope and identity. Generate only one complete event, never a montage or multiple examples. End cleanly in silence with no voice, music, alarm, ambience, background combat, repeated crackles, falling debris sequence, or lingering electrical drone.
